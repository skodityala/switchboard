Two problems that every other product forces into conflict

A 3-provider independent practice cannot staff a phone line from 8am to 6pm. It also cannot absorb a $50,000 HIPAA settlement. Today it has to pick.

Option 1 — voicemail

Free, and loses patients

Refill requests, appointment changes and billing questions go unanswered until someone has time. Callers hang up and call a bigger practice.

Option 2 — answering service

$600–1,200/month, still can't help

Staffed by people who cannot see the chart and are not on your BAA. They take messages. They cannot tell a caller whether a refill is ready.

Option 3 — an AI receptionist

Creates the compliance problem

Hand a model database access, then write a paragraph instructing it to be careful. Instructions are not a security boundary — and your compliance reviewer knows it, which is where the pilot stalls.

Why this matters commercially

That column is called subscriber_key, and it was classified OPERATIONAL by the clinic's own staff — they believed it was an opaque identifier. It derives from the last four of the SSN, which derives from the SSN. A keyword filter on "SSN" hands it over, because the column is not called SSN. That is a mistake a real practice makes, caught by architecture rather than vigilance.

What we do not claim

We remove 75% of a comparable stack's variable cost, not all of it. Speech recognition is the one variable cost a real telephony deployment keeps, and the shipped demo takes typed input, so STT is not yet solved in the local adapter. The strategic point is the shape of the cost: a token-priced competitor at $299 goes gross-margin-negative at 1.1× this usage. Their cost scales with calls. Ours does not.
